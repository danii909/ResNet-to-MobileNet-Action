• Noisy constraint: Forced spatial alignment acts as noise, degrading generalization.

• Temporal transferability: Removing spatial attention (β_s=0, β_t=0.1) attenuates the regression.
• Key insight: Global temporal dynamics are more transferable across heterogeneous architectures than localized spatial features.

Advanced Methods

Born-Again Networks (Self-Distillation)

Analysis of knowledge collapse in recursive distillation

Born-Again Networks test iterative self-distillation (MobileNet3D → MobileNet3D), refining the Student by using itself as the teacher for the next generation.

  • Generational Collapse: Each successive iteration produces lower performance, dropping below the initial baseline (59.48%):
    • Gen 0 (Teacher KD T=8): 64.31%
    • Gen 1: Test Top-1 60.98% (-3.33%)
    • Gen 2: Test Top-1 60.43% (-3.88%)
    • Gen 3: Test Top-1 59.21% (-5.10%)
  • Root Cause (No Oracle): The theory assumes a highly competent teacher. A student at 64% produces less informative soft targets; at high temperature, logits flatten to noise.
  • Error Propagation: Errors from the non-oracle teacher are transmitted and recursively reinforced across generations.

Conclusions

Conclusions & Future Work

Summary of results, lessons learned, and perspectives

Key Findings

• Bridging the Gap: Logit KD at T=20 overcomes the edge-model capacity gap, boosting accuracy by +6.37 pp (to 65.85%).
• Edge Efficiency: Cross-Frame Distillation (16f) halves CPU latency (-52.6%) and increases GPU throughput by +68.8%, with only a marginal accuracy loss (-3.17%).

Lessons Learned

The Capacity Gap blocks spatial intermediate feature transfer. High-temperature distillation (T=20) is the most stable guide.

Known Limitations

• Spatial Student bottleneck.
• Over-smoothing on fast periodic actions.
• Recursive collapse without an oracle.

Future Work

Auto-calibration of T to avoid Born-Again collapse; training on flexible temporal boundaries.

Backup Slide — Qualitative Analysis

Detailed Per-Class Analysis (KD T=20 vs Baseline)

Classes most affected (improvements and regressions) by Knowledge Distillation

Why do some classes improve?

  • Fine spatial details: Classes like WallPushups (0% → 65.7%) or HeadMassage benefit from the Teacher's complex filters for capturing contours and subtle movements.
  • Background clutter removal: Distillation forces the Student to ignore irrelevant visual contexts (e.g. PlayingGuitar, +27.9%).

Why do other classes regress?

  • Temporal logit smoothing: In cyclic, fast actions (e.g. JumpingJack -16.2%, TennisSwing -14.3%), high T=20 over-smooths transition signals.
  • Capacity trade-off: Forced to imitate Teacher's spatial features on a low-param network, the Student sacrifices movement generalization.
